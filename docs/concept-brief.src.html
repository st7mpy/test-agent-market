1 · The idea

Most people lose money trading prediction markets by hand; a small set of automated, capital-rich, infrastructure-heavy operators win. The opportunity is to package that edge into a product that ordinary allocators can buy into safely — without the operators having to give away their alpha.

What it is

A marketplace with two sides. Makers (quants / algo developers) publish trading strategies as vaults. Depositors allocate capital into the vaults they trust. Strategies run on platform-hosted, sandboxed infrastructure — never on a user's laptop, never holding the keys. Capital is custodied in the protocol. Makers are paid only when depositors profit, put their own capital at risk first, and are bounded by automated risk and capacity controls.

What it is deliberately NOT

The naïve version — "open-source bots, one-click local deploy, decentralized, the moat is the quant network" — is internally contradictory and fails on four counts. The design below is the corrected form.

Naïve pitchWhy it breaksThis design instead
Open-source strategiesPublic + capacity-limited alpha decays to zero; the best quants never list (market for lemons)Private strategies; verified real-money track record is the public artifact

One-line model. Makers earn a profit-only performance fee (HWM, tiered by TVL), post first-loss co-investment + a slashable bond, and live under mechanical slashing and dynamic, realized-data capacity caps. Performance is publicly verified; live positions are disclosed on a lag to protect alpha. Custody is split: on-chain ERC-4626 vaults for Polymarket, a regulated custodial vehicle for Kalshi.

2 · Who it is for

A two-sided market. Each side has a sharp, currently-unmet need.

Supply — Makers (quants)

Independent quants, small algo shops, ex-prop / DeFi-native traders who have edge but lack capital, infrastructure, and distribution.

  • Want to monetize a strategy without open-sourcing it
  • Want other people's capital + hosted execution + connectivity
  • Want a portable, verifiable track record

Today they either run tiny personal size or join a fund and lose ownership.

Demand — Depositors (allocators)

Crypto-native retail & HNW, yield-seekers, and small funds / family offices wanting uncorrelated, event-driven returns without building a trading desk.

  • The ~90% who lose trading prediction markets by hand
  • DeFi yield allocators seeking an alternative return stream
  • Want vetted, verified, risk-controlled, passive exposure

Today their only options are gamble manually or trust an unverified Discord "signal".

3 · Probable TAM

Prediction markets are growing fast, but the revenue here is bounded by a hard ceiling: trading profit in these markets is zero-sum and capacity-constrained. The pie is the extractable alpha pool, and it grows only as fast as volume, open interest, and "dumb-flow" supply. Honest sizing below.

Model: revenue = AUM × net alpha × fee take

The platform earns a protocol cut of a profit-only performance fee. The chain is: extractable alpha pool → AUM that captures it → performance fee → protocol take.

Assumptions: managed/allocator capital ≈ 5–20% of open interest; net alpha to depositors ≈ 10–15%/yr for surviving, capacity-capped strategies; blended performance fee ≈ 15%; protocol take ≈ 15–20% of fees. Ranges, not forecasts.

The honest caveat. This is a focused, capacity-bound niche, not a hyperscale market — at today's open interest the whole industry's extractable profit is in the low hundreds of millions. It becomes interesting only on the bet that prediction-market volume and OI keep compounding (monthly volume rose from <$5B in Sep'25 to ~$24B by Apr'26). The model scales with the venues, and is gated by capacity caps that deliberately cap AUM to protect returns.

4 · Architecture

Configuration B-on-C: an infrastructure layer (connectivity, hosted execution, risk, custody, track-record) with a capital-allocator / vault layer on top. Shaped by one rule — untrusted strategy, trusted platform.

The security model — the crux

Strategy code is untrusted. It runs in an isolated microVM with no key access and no network egress beyond the data feed. It can only emit intents (desired orders / target positions). Every intent passes a single Risk Gate choke point; only the Execution/OMS — which alone holds keys in a KMS/HSM — signs and routes orders. This neutralizes the "third-party code near my funds" rug risk that sinks the deploy-locally design.

Vault & fee mechanics (capital alignment)

Capital is tranched: depositor (senior) · maker co-investment (junior / first-loss) · maker bond (locked, slashable). Makers earn a profit-only fee above a per-share high-water mark, tiered by TVL; the co-invest ratio is enforced continuously so alignment can't be diluted as the vault grows. The bond is sized to outweigh the fee's "free option," killing the incentive to take reckless risk.

Custody split & transparency

Polymarket capital sits in on-chain ERC-4626 vaults; Kalshi (off-chain, KYC'd, USD) requires a regulated custodial vehicle. A unified accounting service consolidates both and reconciles NAV against venue reality (divergence → kill-switch). Verified results are public immediately; live positions are disclosed on a lag to prevent copy-trading from decaying the alpha.

5 · User flows

Maker (quant) flow

1
Onboard & KYC. Sign up, identity / sanctions checks, accept maker terms.
2
Author & backtest. Build the strategy in the Maker SDK (Python/TS); run a reproducible, content-addressed backtest against snapshotted historical data.
3
Incubate. Deploy to an incubating vault with only the maker's own capital — no outside AUM yet. Trades run hosted & sandboxed.
4
Earn a verified track record. Real-money PnL is signed and anchored on-chain (e.g. 60-day minimum) — no backtests count toward eligibility.
5
Unlock outside AUM. Post the required co-investment (first-loss) + slashable bond; the vault lists publicly with its verified record, capacity cap, and fee tier.
6
Operate & earn. Collect a profit-only fee above the high-water mark. Stay inside risk limits & capacity caps — breaches trigger mechanical slashing. Graduate to higher TVL tiers as the record lengthens.

Depositor (allocator) flow

1
Browse the marketplace. Filter vaults by verified performance, Sharpe, max drawdown, capacity headroom, maker co-investment, and fee tier.
2
Diligence a vault. Inspect the signed real-money track record, lagged position history, risk mandate, and how much skin the maker has in the game.
3
Pass compliance. KYC + geofencing route the depositor to the correct rail (on-chain Polymarket vs regulated Kalshi vehicle).
4
Deposit. Funds mint vault shares at the current NAV/share — subject to the capacity cap (excess is rejected or queued).
5
Monitor. Track NAV, returns, drawdown, and delayed positions live; receive risk-event and slashing notifications.
6
Withdraw. Redeem shares at NAV after a notice period (positions may be illiquid until event resolution). Performance fee crystallizes only on profit above the high-water mark.
Trust loop. Makers can't touch keys or depositor funds; depositors can't see a strategy's live hand in time to copy it; the platform reconciles every vault's NAV to the venue and halts on divergence. Each side gets what it needs without exposing the other.

6 · Feasibility & risks

Verdict: Conditionally feasible. The software is buildable by a small team in months; the hard parts are economics, alignment, and regulation — all addressable with the design above, but bounded by three conditions.

DimensionStatusNote
DemandStrongReal & compounding; the least of the problems
Value captureSolvedProfit-only fee, enforceable via in-protocol custody
Incentive alignmentSolved**if co-invest ratio + bond are sized right — the #1 design task
Adverse selection (lemons)MitigatedFirst-loss + slashing + verified record self-select genuine edge
Economics at scaleBoundedZero-sum / small-OI ceiling caps total TAM
RegulatoryHeavyProfit-fee on pooled capital ⇒ CPO/CTA wrapper, front-loaded

The three binding conditions

  1. Co-investment ratio + bond sizing must provably outweigh the performance fee's option value — model this first; get it wrong and you've built a blow-up machine.
  2. TAM is capped by open interest (~$0.2–0.3B/venue today). Caps protect depositors but bound revenue; the thesis rides on continued market growth.
  3. A regulated wrapper (CPO/CTA + custodial Kalshi rail, geofenced Polymarket rail) is mandatory and front-loaded.
